Planning a day out here

Livingston sits between two very different kinds of day out. Dale Hollow Lake is north of town, all clear water, shoreline and boat ramps. The waterfall and gorge country runs south and west toward Cookeville, where the creeks have cut down through the limestone. Both are inside about 45 minutes, so you can pick your day by the weather rather than by the drive.

Cell service is unreliable in the deep gorges and on parts of the lake. You should download offline maps before you leave home. Relying on live data can leave you without navigation if you lose signal in the hollows.

Most of the good water is located at the bottom of a hill. You will need to plan for the walk back up after you have finished swimming or exploring the creek beds. The return trip is often steeper and more tiring than the descent.

Gas, food, and ice are easier to get in town before you head out. You will find fewer options for supplies at the destinations themselves. It is better to stock up in Livingston so you do not have to turn back for essentials.

What the seasons actually do

Spring brings high water to the region. The waterfalls run hard during this time, and creek crossings can be dangerous after rain. The volume of water makes some paths slippery and difficult to navigate.

Summer is the main lake season. Swimming holes get crowded on weekends, and the heat and humidity can be intense. You should go early in the day to find space and avoid the peak heat.

Fall is the reliable season for visitors. You will see color on the plateau, and the low water makes gorge walking easier. The conditions are generally stable and comfortable for outdoor activities.

Winter is quiet in this area. Some seasonal facilities close for the colder months. The bare trees open up the overlooks, providing clear views of the landscape despite the lack of foliage.

Before you go

  • Shoes with real tread. Wet limestone is slick even when the trail looks easy.
  • More water than you think you need, especially for the climb back out.
  • Offline maps downloaded before you leave the house.
  • A look at the official page the morning you go, for closures and conditions.
  • A check on how much rain fell upstream. Creeks here come up fast.
  • Sunscreen and bug spray from spring through early fall.
  • A bag for your trash. Most of these places have no cans at the trailhead.
  • Somebody at home who knows which trailhead you parked at.
