Repair & flip — the winter engine

Buy broken game consoles cheap, fix the one fault they nearly all share, resell. Added Jul 2026 after the GPU-repair research: consoles (PS5 HDMI ports, controller stick drift) are the predictable money, because the fault is mechanical and visible in listing photos before you buy. GPUs stay opportunistic high-ticket snipes, taken only when the fault is visibly fixable or the seller offers free returns. Runs at ~8–10 hrs/week inside the 20-hr budget, and it is counter-seasonal to Tapestry: its Q4 selling peak and Jan–Feb sourcing season land exactly in the farm's dark months.

The seasonal rhythm is the whole strategy. Q4 (Oct–Dec) is the selling peak, roughly 2× volume at full prices. Jan–Feb is dead for selling: post-holiday wallets are empty, credit-card bills land, everyone who wanted a console got one for Christmas, and returned/unwanted-gift units flood supply (sold prices dip 10–20%). But that same glut makes Jan–Feb the cheapest buying window of the year for broken units. So the dark months become the acquisition-and-bench season: stack inventory at trough prices, repair through the winter, sell into the tax-refund demand wave (late Feb–Apr) at recovered prices. Buy in the trough, sell into the spike, and all of it happens while the farm is closed.

What it earns inside this plan. At ~8–10 hrs/week: 2–3 consoles plus a controller batch per week ≈ $1,000–1,400/mo steady, spiking past $2k in Q4 and easing off (by design) during Tapestry's open-season crunch. Run full-time instead, it models at ~$45k/yr flipping alone, or ~$70–85k with a local repair-service lane: the fallback job if the software lines stall. The service upgrade path is the real $/hr play regardless, since a mail-in/local HDMI job bills $120–190 against a $10 part with zero inventory float. Startup: ~$400 of tools + ~$2k revolving float, self-funding after month one.

Why 20 hrs/week forces this exact shape

Service work can't get you there. Drone, photography, print/laser, repair flips, and web builds are billed by your time. Inside 20 hrs/week they top out around $8–10k/mo combined — real money, and enough to pay the bills and retire Dani, but a hard ceiling. Every hour spent shooting or flying is an hour not spent building the businesses.

Only three lines scale past that: ORP (near-passive at 75–89% margin), the Backline franchise suite (recurring B2B — one sale = a location that pays every month), and Obscura SaaS (self-serve subscriptions). These earn while you sleep, so the plan pours your limited hours into building and selling them, not into more service labor.

The catch, stated plainly: holding $30k/mo inside 20 hrs/week is only possible if the parts that scale with revenue — ORP fulfillment + content, customer support, Backline onboarding — are absorbed by the Orion AI workforce and a contractor/VA. Without that delegation, $30k/mo needs 40–50 hours. The AI company you already built is the thing that makes the time budget survivable.

Assumptions & honest caveats

  • This is a stretch plan, not a forecast. $30k/mo in 24 months requires the B2B and e-commerce lines to actually scale — 40+ Backline locations, ORP ranking and converting, a real Obscura subscriber base. Each is plausible; all three hitting on schedule is the optimistic path.
  • ORP now has real traffic research behind it: ranked competitors do $330k–$1M+/mo, and the former leader (Peptide Sciences) did ~$7.4M/mo before voluntarily shutting down March 2026 — a demand vacuum a new US vendor can capture. ORP's base case reaches ~$21k/mo by month 24 on just ~6,000 visits/mo, a fraction of any competitor, so its ceiling is traffic + trust, not market size. Two flags: the climb still depends on SEO ranking (slow, 6–12 mo) and it grows to ~half the $30k — a concentration + regulatory risk (a top vendor just chose to shut down). Backline + Obscura are the deliberate diversification against it.
  • ORP's diversification is now researched — a cosmetic peptide skincare brand: 4 SKUs (GHK-Cu copper serum, Matrixyl, Argireline, peptide eye cream), white-labeled (Nardo's, ~100-unit MOQ), ~$5 COGS at $38–58 retail = ~90% gross margin, MoCRA-registered, claims kept appearance-only. Base case ~$10–15k/mo by month 18 off the existing ORP email list. It makes the ORP contribution legal, durable, and less concentration-prone — the hedge against the RUO regulatory risk. Systemic/edible research peptides stay off the table as unapproved drugs.
  • The 20-hr/week cap only holds with delegation. The plan explicitly leans on the Orion workforce + a contractor/VA for the revenue-scaling grunt work. Without that, the same income needs roughly double the hours.
  • Tapestry's ~$271k is a target buildout, anchored to a real proof point. Whispering Oaks did ~$80k/yr on the store + walks alone; this model rebuilds that floor and layers weddings, glamping, and catering on top. It's seasonal (7 open months) and labor-heavy while open, and the wedding + glamping figures assume the full 6-unit buildout and a booked 2-weddings-a-month calendar — those are the two swing factors. The store + walks (~$75k) are the safe floor; the events are the upside. Silkie breeding (~$10k) needs NPIP certification + biosecurity to ship birds or eggs across state lines. Folding into the timeline: the funding math counts your 33% profit share (a three-way with Tari & Dale). Margins run ~50% on sold products but ~75–85% on weddings + experiences, so the blend is ~65% net (~$180k/yr profit) — your share ~$60k/yr at buildout, ~$25k in 2027, which pulls the finish to ~2030; the $30k/mo run-rate curve itself stays software-only.
  • Taxes + reinvestment are in the surplus math (~25–30% effective, ~10% back into the business). Phase-2 dates use net-of-both surplus over ~$3,500/mo living costs.
  • The house (~Year 3–4) is the plan's tail — funded by accumulated surplus once income holds at $30k+/mo, plus the farm profit share. The 24-month sprint builds the machine; 2027–2030 pays off the goal list (the $55k Beyond Finance debt first, the house last). The finish swings a year either way with how income and taxes land.
  • The repair-flip lane rides on eBay's rails. New-seller limits and payout holds throttle the first 60–90 days, a suspended account is a stopped lane, and the first month of consoles includes tuition units (a lifted pad kills the board). Margins assume category-norm success rates after ~20 practice units. The local service lane is the hedge, and figures here are the part-time (~8–10 hr/wk) case, not the full-time model.
  • Two inputs still refine this: the ORP traffic ceiling, and whether Backline can move as fast through the GC franchise network as modeled. Both move the $30k date more than anything else.
